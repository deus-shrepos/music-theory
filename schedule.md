## YOUR 1-HOUR DAILY GUITAR SCHEDULE

### Every single day

|Time|What you do|
|---|---|
|**5 min**|Tune + setup|
|**10 min**|Technique|
|**15 min**|Main lesson/book|
|**10 min**|Chords / rhythm|
|**15 min**|Song practice|
|**5 min**|Free playing / review|

**Total: 60 minutes.**

Don't make yourselves do 60 minutes of boring exercises. The **song section is mandatory** because that's the part that makes you actually want to pick up the guitar tomorrow.

# 📚 THE BOOK STACK

You do **NOT** need 10 books.

### 1. Main method

**Hal Leonard Guitar Method – Book 1**

This is your structured textbook. It covers fundamentals, notation, rhythm, chords and first-position playing. The current second edition also comes with online audio.

[Hal Leonard Guitar Method – Book 1](https://www.halleonard.com/product/699010/hal-leonard-guitar-method-book-1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2. Electric-guitar-specific book

**First 15 Lessons – Electric Guitar — Troy Nelson**

This is particularly useful for you because you're not trying to become acoustic folk players 😂. It introduces electric-guitar techniques such as:

- alternate picking
- palm muting
- power chords
- hammer-ons
- pull-offs
- slides
- bends
- vibrato
- harmonics
- scales
- basic theory

It also uses actual songs, including **Smoke on the Water, Smells Like Teen Spirit, You Really Got Me, Under the Bridge, When I Come Around**, etc.

[First 15 Lessons – Electric Guitar](https://www.halleonard.com/product/244589/first-15-lessons-electric-guitar?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 3. Free online course

**JustinGuitar Beginner Grade 1**

Use this as your **video teacher** rather than trying to learn everything from books.

[JustinGuitar Beginner Course](https://www.justinguitar.com/beginner?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Justin's course is deliberately incremental: essential chords → chord changes → strumming → songs → more techniques.

# 🗓️ THE FIRST 8 WEEKS

## WEEK 1 — "WHAT THE HELL ARE WE DOING"

**Goal:** Become comfortable holding and controlling the guitar.

### Monday

- Tune guitar
- Parts of the guitar
- Correct sitting/standing position
- Pick holding
- Downstrokes
- Play open strings

### Tuesday

- Downstrokes
- Alternate picking
- E, A, D strings
- Simple one-string melodies

### Wednesday

- First notes
- Fretting correctly
- Avoiding buzzing
- Simple rhythm exercises

### Thursday

- Metronome
- Quarter notes
- Eighth notes
- Simple riffs

### Friday

- Review everything

### Saturday

🎵 **Song day**

Start a very easy riff/song.

### Sunday

**Fun day**

No new material.

Play whatever you know.

---

# WEEK 2 — CHORDS

Learn:

**Em → A → D → E → Am**

Don't try to learn 15 chords at once.

Your 15-minute chord block becomes:

> Make chord → strum → stop → change chord → repeat.

Start measuring **clean chord changes**, not speed.

---

# WEEK 3 — RHYTHM

Now you're becoming guitar players rather than people holding guitars. 😭

Work on:

- downstrokes
- alternate strumming
- quarter notes
- eighth notes
- metronome
- changing chords without stopping

Start playing **whole songs**, even if they're painfully slow.

---

# WEEK 4 — POWER CHORDS 🔥

This is where electric guitar starts feeling like **electric guitar**.

Learn:

- E5
- A5
- D5
- moving power-chord shapes
- palm muting
- eighth-note riffs

This gives you the foundation for a LOT of rock.

---

# WEEK 5 — LEAD GUITAR

Start:

- single-string melodies
- minor pentatonic scale
- alternate picking
- simple riffs
- basic improvisation

You don't need to become a shredder.

Just learn to move around the fretboard.

---

# WEEK 6 — ELECTRIC GUITAR TECHNIQUES

Start introducing:

**hammer-ons**

**pull-offs**

**slides**

**bends**

**vibrato**

Do **not** rush these.

A clean bend that sounds good is more valuable than doing 50 ugly bends.

---

# WEEK 7 — PLAYING TOGETHER

THIS IS IMPORTANT BECAUSE YOU HAVE A GUITAR FRIEND.

One person plays:

> **Chords**

The other plays:

> **Riff / melody**

Then switch.

Example:

**You:** power chords  
**Friend:** pentatonic riff

Then:

**Friend:** chords  
**You:** melody

This teaches you something books can't really reproduce: **playing with another human being.**

---

# WEEK 8 — MINI PERFORMANCE

At the end of Week 8, choose **3 songs**.

For each:

### Song 1

Easy chords.

### Song 2

Power chords / rock.

### Song 3

Riff or melody.

Then record yourselves playing them.

Not because you're posting them.

Just so you can compare:

**Day 1 → Week 8.**

You'll probably be shocked by the difference.

## 📖 About finding the books online

One of the Hal Leonard editions is catalogued by Open Library with an **Internet Archive item record**, so you may encounter it there.

For copyrighted books, though, I'd stick to **Internet Archive lending/access where the book is legitimately available**, rather than random PDF mirrors.

You could also use your **Obsidian + GitHub setup** as the shared _study system_: keep your schedule, notes, chord diagrams you create yourselves, practice logs, song lists, and theory notes in the repo, while keeping copyrighted book PDFs only where you're legally allowed to share them.
